Turning vacancy risk into conversion urgency

Every empty habi costs Enso ~4.2K€/month. Our listing cards were static: a room available tomorrow looked identical to one available in three months. We built a dynamic system that progressively escalates urgency signals based on real-time vacancy risk, driving action without feeling manipulative.

01 Problem

Every empty room is burning money

Enso's coliving model operates on occupancy. When a habi sits empty past its target move-in date, the company absorbs rent, utilities, and maintenance with zero revenue. In Q3 alone, 23% of rooms exceeded their target date.

The core issue: our listing page treated every room equally. A high-risk vacancy that needed to fill this week displayed the same card as a room available three months out.

HypothesisIf we surface the right urgency signals at the right time, we can accelerate bookings for high-risk inventory without degrading trust.
02 Approach

Design principles for ethical urgency

Urgency design is a minefield. Booking.com-style dark patterns erode trust. We needed a framework that drove action while remaining honest.

Design rationaleProgressive disclosure: start clean, only add elements when business risk justifies them. Every signal is factual. Viewer counts are live sessions, not fabricated. Discounts are real. The user decides. We surface context. We never manufacture panic.
02b Service Blueprint

How the system works across layers

The urgency system operates across four layers: the user-facing experience, the business logic engine, the data pipeline, and the operational feedback loop. Each layer feeds the next.

03 Framework

Risk-calibrated card system

Five risk tiers drive the progressive addition of urgency elements. Each tier maps to days remaining before a room's vacancy becomes a financial liability.

04 Signal Logic

Element activation matrix

Each UI element activates at a specific risk tier. The system is additive: higher tiers inherit all elements from lower tiers plus new ones.

07 Validation

A/B experiment: Dynamic Urgency Signals v1

PostHog controlled experiment: 14,208 unique users, 50/50 split, 28 days.

Card Click-ThroughControl: 8.3%. Variant: 12.1%. +45.8% uplift, p < 0.001
Booking ConversionControl: 2.4%. Variant: 3.2%. +33.8% uplift, p = 0.003
Median Time to BookControl: 6.2 days. Variant: 3.8 days. -38.7% reduction, p = 0.008
08 Funnel Analysis

Where the uplift compounded

Card click-through was the primary lever (+45.8%), but gains compounded downstream: room view rate +11.7%, checkout start +12.9%. The urgency signals attracted higher-intent clicks.

09 Segmentation

Breakdown by city, device, and risk level

Mobile showed the highest uplift (+50%). And Level 4 drove +86% uplift, confirming that stacking perks at high-risk tiers materially accelerates conversion.

10 Experiment Rigor

Statistical significance over time

95% significance on Day 16. Ran full 28 days. No novelty decay. Uplift was behavioral, not curiosity-driven.

11 Guardrails

Monitoring for unintended harm

  • Early cancellation rate: Control 4.8%, Variant 4.6%. No increase.
  • Support ticket volume: Control 12/wk, Variant 11/wk. Slight decrease.
  • NPS post-booking: Control 62, Variant 65. Slight improvement.
  • Average discount depth: 13.6%. Within margin. Monitoring ongoing.
12 Reflection

What I learned

Why "scarcity, not pressure"Early explorations had countdown timers. 4/5 testers said "this feels like Booking.com." Trust > urgency. Always. Show what's real instead of manufacturing time pressure.
What I'd do differentlyStart mobile-only (73% of traffic). Track cross-device journeys from Day 1. A/B test individual signals, not the full bundle.
