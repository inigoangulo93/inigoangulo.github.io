02 The Real Problem

v1 was a terminal log with 23 metrics per pair.

Complete information, zero decision clarity. At 2am with leveraged capital moving against you, the question isn't analytical — it's binary: "Am I okay right now?" The problem was never information. It was architecture.

  • Signals are ambiguous by nature. Non-stationary data, regime shifts, correlation breakdowns. Hesitation has a cost. Overreaction has a cost.
  • Latency is structural. 60-second tick, rate-limited APIs, two-leg execution. A signal valid 30 seconds ago may not be valid now.
  • Emotional bias compounds faster than losses. After a drawdown: impulse to recover. After a streak: impulse to increase size. Both destroy accounts.
v1 terminal output: 23 metrics per pair
03 Key Insight

Most systems fail not because of bad signals. They fail because of bad decision architecture.

The entire 23-metric pipeline needed to collapse into one question: what's the z-score? Entry at 2.4σ, exit at 0.8σ, stop at 4.5σ. At 2am, one glance. Not a lecture.

Evidence v1 had perfect information — 23 metrics — and I couldn't act. v2 has a gate that decides autonomously and only surfaces what requires intervention. The shift: from "here's all the data" to "the system is handling it, here's what needs your attention."
04 What I Changed

Three design interventions. From raw data to decision architecture.

1. Designed a 5-layer decision architecture

Signal emergence → validation → decision → execution → survival. Each layer has one responsibility.

5-layer decision architecture diagram

2. Built a 5-priority deterministic decision gate

Priority 1: Survival (kill switch, sticky, manual reset). Priority 2: Execution health (slippage monitoring). Priority 3: Model validity (shadow trader divergence). Priority 4: Edge exists (rolling edge tracker). Priority 5: Standard checks (z-score, risk, limits). Higher priorities override everything below.

Priority cascade: deterministic decision gate

3. Two operator surfaces: Dashboard + Telegram

Dashboard for analysis (5 views: OPS, LOG, CTRL, RADAR, TERM). Telegram for intervention (3 tiers: Read, Write, Destructive with 2-step kill). 95% of interaction is one question answered via Telegram.

Dashboard views and Telegram command tiers
05 Key Decisions

What trade-offs did I make?

Every decision involved choosing between flexibility and determinism under capital risk. These were the most consequential:

DecisionChosenRejectedWhy
Gate logic Deterministic cascade Probabilistic model Under capital risk, "73% confident" is not a useful post-mortem. Hard thresholds = complete traceability.
Kill switch Sticky (manual reset + 10 recovery trades) Auto-reset Prevents re-entry after drawdown driven by emotional bias.
Primary surface Telegram Dashboard 95% of interaction is "am I okay?" — phone notification answers it in 5 seconds.
Infrastructure Local-only (no cloud) Cloud deployment Zero execution latency, complete runtime control. Third-party infra fails at worst possible moment.

07 Takeaway

Design for the worst moment, not the average one. The interface you check at 2am is the real product.

The pattern Better signals don't fix bad decision systems. Most data-heavy interfaces fail because they provide information instead of decisions. The primary surface is always the one used at lowest attention, not the one with the most features.
What failed v1 dashboard was beautiful — detailed charts, full statistics. Completely useless at 2am. Redesign inverted hierarchy: system state first (GO/PAUSED/KILLED), not data. Also: pocket-dialed /kill once during a profitable run — added 2-step confirmation after. Also underestimated reconciliation — phantom positions from network timeouts during execution.
