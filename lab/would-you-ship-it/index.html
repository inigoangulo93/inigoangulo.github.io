    All six decisions
    

    All experiments were A/B tests with statistical significance thresholds, 120K to 140K users per variant across the programme, guardrail metrics tracked throughout. Figures are directional reconstructions of relative effects; exact values are confidential.
    

    1. EXP-01
      Hold-your-spot countdown: "We'll hold your spot for 30:00"
      Context. Travellers reached the total, doubted the price and looped back to check it. 38% of eventual converters left checkout mid loop. We expected: Showing the real hold time would turn urgency into reassurance at the moment of paying.
      Evidence. Payment completion +3.1%; Urgency related support tickets: zero.
      Decision: Shipped. Urgency reframed as service: the spot is reserved for the traveller, not used against them. It was the top single win of the programme.
      Urgency builds trust only when it is true and on the user's side.
    2. EXP-06
      Price-expiry countdown: "Book now, the price rises when the timer ends"
      Context. The hold-your-spot timer (EXP-01) was already live. This tested the opposite framing of the same mechanic. We expected: A price deadline would push hesitant travellers to pay sooner.
      Evidence. Click through to payment +4.2%; Trust survey −9 pts; Refund intent proxy: up.
      Decision: Killed. The click was won, but trust and refund intent moved the wrong way. A win that spends trust is a loss with a delay. Killed on principle and on data.
      Not every conversion gain creates business value.
    3. EXP-07
      Promo code as a quiet link instead of an open field
      Context. An open promo code field sat in the middle of checkout, just before paying. We expected: A quiet link would stop mid checkout coupon hunting without hiding the option.
      Evidence. Exits to hunt for coupons −31%; Net checkout completion: up.
      Decision: Shipped. An empty field is an invitation to leave and go coupon hunting. A link keeps the option without issuing the invitation.
      Remove the invitation to leave, not the option.
    4. EXP-08
      Single-page checkout: collapse every step into one view
      Context. On mobile each step was a separate page load, and every transition cost 8 to 12% of remaining users. We expected: If reloads broke the flow, removing the steps altogether would remove the leak.
      Evidence. Desktop conversion +0.4% (not significant); Mobile conversion −0.9%; Render time, mid range Android: about +1.2 s.
      Decision: Killed. You do not trade your best segment for elegance. The idea was killed, but its principle survived: unbroken context, shipped as two named steps.
      Read the segment that carries the result, not the average.
    5. EXP-09
      Email-first continue, account creation deferred
      Context. New travellers met a create account step before they could pay. We expected: Asking only for an email to continue would lift new user conversion.
      Evidence. New user conversion, v1 flat (not significant); Session recordings: still hesitating at "create account".
      Decision: Iterated. The number was flat, but the qualitative signal survived. v2 paired email first with SSO and moved account creation after purchase: +1.1%, shipped.
      Flat is not dead when the qualitative signal survives.
    6. EXP-10
      Reorder payment methods to match each market
      Context. Credit card showed first in every market. In the Netherlands 72% of online payments use iDEAL; in Poland, BLIK dominates. We expected: Leading with each market's preferred method would lift payment completion.
      Evidence. 2 of 4 markets +1.7%; The other 2 markets flat.
      Decision: Iterated. Not a global truth, a local one. It became a per market rollout with a shared default, instead of forcing one answer on four markets.
      A local truth deserves a local rollout.
