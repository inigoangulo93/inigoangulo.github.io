Fractional product manager vs product consultant: which one do you actually need?

Inigo AnguloNovember 4, 20265 min readProduct and consulting
In this series: Product and consulting
  • How much does it cost to build an MVP with AI agents? Real numbers from one build

The two titles get used interchangeably, and companies hire the wrong one all the time: a consultant when they needed someone to own the roadmap, a fractional lead when they needed a second opinion. The difference is not seniority or price. It is who owns the outcome.

The consultant

Comes in with a question, leaves with an answer. Audits the funnel, reviews the roadmap, runs the workshop, writes the recommendation. Owns the quality of the diagnosis; does not own what happens next. Right when you have a specific question, a team that can execute, and a decision maker who will act on the answer. Wrong when the answer is "you need someone to run this", because the consultant is already gone.

The fractional product manager

Sits inside the team for two or three days a week over months, owns the roadmap and the outcomes, makes the daily calls, and is judged on whether the number moved. Right when the founder is still the product manager and it has stopped scaling, when you are between hires, or when the product function does not exist yet and someone has to build it. Wrong when what you need is a deck for the board, or when nobody on your side will own the product after they leave.

Four questions that settle it

  1. Is there a decision maker who will act on a recommendation? If yes, a consultant works. If the recommendation would land on nobody's desk, you need the fractional lead.
  2. Is the problem known? "Checkout converts badly" is a consultant's problem. "We do not know what to build next quarter" is a fractional lead's.
  3. How long until the next hire? If a full time product lead starts in two months, a consultant bridges. If the search has not started, the fractional lead is the bridge and often helps run the search.
  4. Who executes? A consultant assumes your team ships. If your team is two engineers and no designer, the fractional lead needs to be able to do, not just direct.

What each should cost

A consulting engagement is priced per deliverable: an audit, a plan, a workshop series. A fractional engagement is priced per days per week, with a minimum term of three months, because owning an outcome in less than that is theatre. Both should come with a first call where the person tells you honestly which one you need, including when it is neither.
